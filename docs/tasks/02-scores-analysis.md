## 5. Feasibility: 30 parts cut between phrases

A quick check for Task 3, not a proposal. Phrases are grouped into 30 consecutive parts by dynamic programming, minimising each part's squared deviation from 1/30 of the total. This is done once balancing by recitation and once by typesetting, and both scores are reported for each result.

Parts hold 28–91 phrases (median 36); the parts in Juz ʿAmma hold the most, as its phrases are short. The largest phrase is 8.6% of a part by recitation (8.4% by typesetting), which limits how exactly a cut can land. Even so, every part ends within 2.6% of the target by recitation (3.5% when balancing by typesetting).

## 6. Takeaways

- The traditional juz vary by CV 0.033 in recitation (longest/shortest 1.12×). Cutting only at phrase boundaries brings that to CV 0.0080 (1.042×), so meaningful boundaries cost almost nothing in balance.
- Recitation and typesetting correlate strongly (phrase Pearson 0.994), but balancing only one leaves the other at CV about 0.03: parts balanced for the page range 1.15× in recitation, wider than the traditional juz. Serving reciters and typesetters together needs a weighted mix of the two scores, which is a decision for Task 3.
- Surah openings (title and basmala) add fixed costs that the per-aya scores leave out; Task 3 must count them, as done here for the juz totals.
