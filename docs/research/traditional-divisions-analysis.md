# The traditional rubʿ, measured

Generated by `scripts/analyze_traditional.py`; do not edit by hand. The method is the one al-Ṣafāqusī states in *Ghayth al-Nafʿ* (see the [README](../../README.md) and [the study](ghayth-al-naf.md)). The data are the Madina Mushaf rubʿ starts (`data/source/hafs-rub-start-ayahs.json`). Letters are the written letters of the Tanzil Uthmani text without the basmala: 325,386 in all.

## 1. Drawn at stops, not by count

For each division: how far its starts lie from the equal-letter positions (k/30, k/60 or k/240 of the letters), in units of that division. The second column is the distance that aya ends alone force, i.e. the distance to the nearest aya end.


| division | mean distance from equal position | forced by aya ends | excess | longest/shortest (letters) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| juz | 2.4% | 0.18% | 14× | 1.18× |
| hizb | 5.8% | 0.36% | 16× | 1.36× |
| rubʿ | 22.6% | 1.54% | 15× | 2.16× |
| rubʿ, inside its own juz | 14.9% | 1.49% | 10× | – |


The juz lie close to equal-letter positions: 2.4% of a juz on average. Inside a juz, the rubʿ lie 15% of a rubʿ from equal positions, 10 times further than aya ends would force. Their lengths vary by 2.2× in letters. This is what al-Ṣafāqusī describes: he does not compute the quarters. He takes the agreed or well-known ones and ends each at a proper stop. The quarters were drawn where a thought ends, not by count.

## 2. But many quarters do not start where a thought starts

The stated rule is that every quarter ends at a complete stop (*waqf tāmm*): what follows does not depend on it, in wording or in meaning. Two checks show where the transmitted boundaries break that rule. The first is against our reading of the text, the second against the wording alone:

- **Inside a phrase.** The start falls inside one of the Task 1 subject phrases. Those are the phrases as a reading of the text, with the splits made to fit divisions merged back. The aya before the boundary and the aya after it belong to one subject.
- **Leaning start.** The first word ties the aya to the one before it: a reply («قال»، «قالوا»، «قالت»), or «ف»، «ثم»، «إذ»، «حتى». Such a start continues a sentence, a story or a dialogue. The check is crude: a resumptive «ف» (as in «فاصدع بما تؤمر») opens a new subject, so a flag is a prompt to read the text, not a verdict.


| boundary | inside a phrase | median depth into the phrase | leaning start |
|---|---|---|---|
| juz starts (29) | 11 (38%) | 38% | 5 |
| second-hizb starts (30) | 12 (40%) | 60% | 3 |
| other rubʿ starts (180) | 67 (37%) | 50% | 13 |
| all 239 | 90 (38%) | 50% | 21 |

## 3. The issue

The rule is right. Al-Ṣafāqusī ends each quarter at a proper stop, so that the unit is complete in meaning. The transmitted quarters follow it only partly:

1. **Uneven.** The quarters are not computed, so their lengths vary by 2.2× in letters and by about 2.2× on recitation and print. A reader's daily portion or a printer's page budget changes with them.
2. **Not at the ends of thoughts after all.** 90 of 239 boundaries fall inside a subject phrase, and 21 open with a reply or a connective. The chosen boundaries do not keep the rule they were chosen by.

Quran-Tajzee applies the same rule to every boundary: a quarter ends at the end of a thought, here the end of a subject phrase. It then chooses among the stops that satisfy the rule by measured length:


| measure | traditional | Quran-Tajzee |
|---|---|---|
| mean distance of a rubʿ start from equal position (within its juz) | 15% | 6% |
| rubʿ starts inside a subject phrase (of 210 inside the juz) | 79 | 0; 57 at reviewed complete stops added in round 2026-10-18 |
| rubʿ starts with a leaning first word (of 210) | 16 | 19 |


**How to read this table.** The phrase test cannot judge the new division: its boundaries are phrase starts by construction, and the phrases are one reading of the text, still under review. The wording test is independent of that reading, and on it the two divisions score alike. Most new flags are a resumptive «ف» at a new subject, such as «فاصدع» (15:94), «فلا تطع» (68:8) and «فإذا جاءت الطامة» (79:34). A few are real: 11:91, 12:77, 26:116 and 27:27 open on a reply inside a dialogue, because a 10-aya phrase limit splits long stories at turns of the dialogue. Two things would settle the meaning question independently. One is the pause grades of a classical *waqf* work (al-Dānī's *al-Muktafā*, al-Ushmūnī's *Manār al-Hudā*) at every candidate aya end. The other is a review of the new starts that open on a reply.
