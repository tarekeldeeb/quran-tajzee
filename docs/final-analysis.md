# Quran-Tajzee — final analysis

Generated by `scripts/final_analysis.py`; do not edit by hand. Sizes are the 50/50 mix of recitation (Hafs harakat with madd, Task 2) and print width (DigitalKhatt, Task 2), with the basmala at surah openings. The phrases are the Task 1 subject phrases, still under review.

## 1. The division

- **Juz:** the traditional 30 of the Madina Mushaf, whose divisions come from al-Ṣafāqusī's *Ghayth al-Nafʿ* ([study](research/ghayth-al-naf.md)). They are kept: optimising them (Task 3) moves the worst juz only from ±6% to ±5%.
- **Hizb:** new. Each juz is cut in two at the phrase start that best halves it, so every hizb opens a subject.
- **Rubʿ:** new. Each hizb is cut into four, again only at phrase starts. Every rubʿ is within ±21% of a quarter of its hizb.

**No new boundary cuts into a phrase.** The traditional hizb and rubʿ start inside a subject phrase 79 times out of 210 (section 4).

Every cut is exact: a dynamic program finds the optimum, and the tests check it against exhaustive search. Data: `data/rub/hizb.tsv`, `data/rub/rub.tsv`.

Traditionally 90 of the 240 rubʿ starts (all levels) fall inside a subject phrase, typically halfway through it, so the new unit opens in the middle of a subject. **In the final division no hizb or rubʿ does:** every boundary it adds opens a phrase. The 11 cases left are traditional juz starts, kept as they are. Splitting those phrases in review makes the whole division phrase-aligned. Every traditional case is listed in `data/rub/traditional-phrase-cuts.tsv`.


6 of the 30 second-hizb starts and 41 of the 180 inner rubʿ starts are unchanged. The final division opens fewer hizb at a surah than the tradition does: the traditional hizb often start at a surah and accept the imbalance (juz 28: ±13.9%). A surah preference for hizb, like the juz penalty of Task 3, would trade some of that balance back.
